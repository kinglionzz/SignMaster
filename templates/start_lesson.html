A Quick History of Nigerian Sign Language

Did You Know?

The NSL emerged from two angles.
The first angle came from American Sign Language (ASL), which itself had borrowed heavily from French Sign Language (LSF) during its early development in the United States. ASL was imported to Nigeria when the Wesley School for the Deaf was established in 1956/57 as an educational tool. In 1960, Pastor Dr. Andrew Foster introduced the missionary dimension to make Wesley both an academic and missionary tool. Deaf graduates and teachers of this school, along with the establishment of the National Association of the Deaf (NAD) now the Nigeria National Association of the Deaf (NNAD) in 1972 further helped spread American Sign Language across Nigeria.
The second angle was the additions that Deaf Nigerians made by inventing their own signs to add to the imported ASL system. Native signs from the Hausa, Yoruba, and Igbo cultures were integrated with the existing ASL to form what is known today as Nigerian Sign Language (NSL).
Various alterations have been made over the years by Deaf and hearing individuals to form a recognized and accepted NSL. Today, advocacy efforts led by the NNAD leadership under Dr. Chidi Olujie, with support from the National Commission for Persons with Disabilities (NCPWD), continue the commendable work of documenting Nigerian Sign Language a language that remains solely and wholly a "Deaf People" affair.
